# Workflow: explainer (notes, a topic, an article → a lesson)

Goal: the viewer understands one idea (or a short chain of ideas) and can
answer 2–3 check questions afterwards. The product entry point: "analyze this
file, extract the main learnings, make an educational animation", done with
verified facts and a storyboard.

## Steps

1. **Read the material and extract.** List the learnings, then pick the one
   the video is about. Everything else is cut or becomes another video
   (≤ 6 min total, ≤ 90 s per segment). Note the source line of every fact.
2. **Level and language.** Who is watching (age, course), which words they
   already know, which word they must leave with.
3. **Choose a shape** (reorder the source; don't follow its paragraph order):

   | Shape | Beats | Good for |
   |---|---|---|
   | concept | hook (why it matters) → the idea in one line → how it works (2–3 builds) → example → recap | definitions, laws, theories |
   | how-to | hook (the result) → steps 1…n → check → recap | procedures, methods |
   | compare | hook → A → B → the one difference that matters → when to use each | pairs of ideas |
   | story | situation → problem → turn → what changed → lesson | history, cases |

4. **Pick the template** (`references/styles.md`): definitions and formulas
   → `editorial-serif`; one idea transforming → `one-shot-morph`; a
   character teaching → `halftone-tutor`; numbers → hand to `data-story`.
   A long lesson can chain templates as separate segments.
5. **Storyboard proposal** (`references/storyboard.md`): the strategy line and
   the beat table. Wait for approval when the user is reviewing.
6. **Script and timing** (`references/audio-voice.md`): `say` verbalized,
   `show` minimal, anchors on the words that name what appears.
7. **Build → check → sheet → render → verify.**
8. **Deliver with the check questions** (2–3, answerable from the video) as
   text, for the platform to show after the video.

## Rules

- Every motion explains something: highlight, annotate, point, zoom, dim the rest, synced to the word.
- Build formulas and diagrams in the order of the reasoning.
- On screen: key terms, formulas, numbers. Never whole sentences of the narration.
- If a fact can't be traced to the material or a computation, it doesn't go in.
